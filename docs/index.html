Have we reached peak romance?

By Lucia Mackenzie

In June 2022 over half of bestselling books in the U.S. were romance novels. It is the first and only time in the last decade that romance books took up the majority of spots in a month of New York Times best seller lists.

That is according to an AI model I asked to comb through over 10 years worth of data.

I began reading romance during the pandemic, consuming both the books and the accompanying TikTok content at speed with my copious amount of freetime.

My friends and I congregated monthly on Zoom to discuss the latest releases in the genre, starting Goodreads and Storygraph accounts to share recommendations and reviews.

As shops opened up again, "booktok made me buy it" tables and shelves were given pride of place in most retailers I visited.

In this analysis I set out to establish how much my personal experience falling in love with romance lines up with broader trends in the genre's popularity

Here is what I found...

Between 2016 and 2025, there were over 8000 best seller list spots available to aspiring authors.

Fewer than 2,200 books populated those spots, about 20 percent of them romance titles.

Where the Crawdads Sing spent almost 200 weeks on the best sellers list over the past decade.

Of the 10 books that appeared on the list the most over the past decade, only 2 were classified as romance novels by my AI model. The most popular is The Seven Husbands of Evelyn Hugo, written by Taylor Jenkins Reid

This classification hides elements of the romance genre's influence in other popular reads. For instance, Fourth Wing and Iron Flame are both romantasy books — fantasy with an integral romance plot.

Similarly, Colleen Hoover's It Ends with Us covers themes of domestic violence by exploring a romantic relationship between two characters.

In fact, several of Hoover's other titles are among the most prominent romance novels from the past decade.

Hoover wrote 3 of the top 10 romance novels, with Emily Henry additionally penning Happy Place and People We Meet on Vacation.

These two authors are a major driver of the spike in romance novels' popularity on the New York Times bestsellers list

In June 2022, the month of peak romance on the best sellers's list over the past decade, each author had 3 seperate books on the list.

While the summer of 2022 to saw a spike in romance authors dominating sales, followed by a drop in their prominance, data from 2026 suggests something of a resurgence for the genre.

Methodology

I queried the New York Times' API to gather the list of print and e-book fiction best sellers for every week between Jan. 2016 and the end of June 2026. I then used the GPT 5.4 Mini AI to classify the 2,137 unique titles in this data set as romance or non-romance novels based on their listed title and description. I refined my prompt to improve the models accuracy when compared to a handcoded sample of 100 titles. The accuracy rate of the prompt used for this analysis was 97%

The analysis has the following limitations:

Classification of books as purely romance or non-romance novels does not allow for more nuanced categories like romantasy to be quantified, and can result in debateable categorisations.

While the model has an accuracy rate of 97 percent, this is based on a small handcoded sample which should be increased in size in future projects.

27 books did not have listed descriptions and were thus not included in the list of 2,137 titles analysed by the AI.
